title: 'Where is the Nuance?'
pubDate: 2026-10-30

From reading this title, you probably think you apply nuance all the time.

> But you would be surprised how many people get stuck in life, or blame problems they created themselves on others. In an almost xenophobic reaction.

I went straight to the point with that one, without any nuance at all. I did that on purpose, to show how easy it is to sound smart without giving any nuance!

Thinking you are nuanced in your opinions, conversations or research is a major trap, and so many people fall into it very easily. To give a bit of context: these are mostly people who are passionate about something and voice their opinions loudly, on social media for example, but haven't actually read up on it at all.

Instead of taking a step back and simply not having an opinion yet, or putting in a bit of effort (and admitting they're an idiot, like me :) ) to read different sources or look for a wide range of opinions...

They grab onto whatever comforts them, instead of the opinion that makes them uncomfortable.

> Like developers facing the fact that AI is faster at coding than they are.

Instead of accepting that their job is at risk, many developers turn hard against AI and reach for all kinds of absurd reasons why their jobs are still justified.

But a more nuanced opinion would be:

> Software developers can do more, more easily, and they will take on very different roles inside companies. They become more entrepreneurs and managers than software developers.

Even this take isn't that nuanced. But you can feel what I'm doing: I'm adding nuance to the speculation that developers will move into a more entrepreneurial role, while still accepting that AI does the coding better than them.

It adds a bit more nuance, and sometimes that is VERY important for context. Take this example:

**"Woman sues McDonald's for millions after spilling coffee on herself"** [Liebeck v. McDonald's Restaurants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liebeck_v._McDonald%27s_Restaurants)

Reading just the title, you would think this woman got a nice bag of money without having to do much.

But in reality, she was 79 years old and got third-degree burns on her groin and thighs. She needed skin grafts and spent 8 days in hospital. That's not something you recover from quickly. And she wasn't looking for millions at all. She only wanted her medical bills covered and offered to settle for $20,000. McDonald's offered $800.

In court, the jury found McDonald's mostly responsible: they served their coffee dangerously hot and had already received over 700 complaints about burns. The "millions" were extra damages meant to punish McDonald's, and the judge later reduced those by a lot.

So as you can see, the title tells you almost nothing about the actual lawsuit. Adding nuance isn't just adding a few words. Sometimes you have to know the whole story before you can even form an opinion.

And that's where the trap is. Once you've read the title and decided how you feel about it, it's very hard to change your mind.

> You've already convinced yourself it should be this way and no other way, and every new piece of information has to fight against that.

And no, I'm not above this. I fall for it too :)

But the older I get, the more I realise that almost nothing in life is as black and white as it first looks. That's also why I think younger people, with less life experience and no professional experience yet, often have a harder time with nuance.

You can even see it in politics. Research shows that people at both extremes, far left and far right, share the same way of thinking: more black and white, and more overconfident in their own judgment ([van Prooijen & Krouwel, 2019](https://research.vu.nl/ws/files/72590886/CDPS_2019_Extremism.pdf)).

They disagree on almost everything, except *how* they think.

Social media only makes it worse. Everything gets squeezed into a title, a 30-second video or a one-liner, and the most extreme take gets the most attention.

Nuance doesn't go viral. Critical thinking takes effort, and sadly, most people don't have to use it in their daily lives.

> Not everything in life is black and white. But it's a lot easier to pretend it is.

So, are you still thinking critically and staying nuanced?

I guess if you've read this far... yes.

Maybe... ;)
